Forge App Directions
Forge · design review · round 2 · 15 Nov 2026

Four directions for the whole app

Round 2 covers all of Forge, not only notes and reminders. Every direction keeps the six tabs, adds one plus button that can add anything, and answers the daily question on Today: did I log my expenses, solve my problems and do my habits? All content is sample data. The check circles work.

Kept from the current appSix tabs in the same order: Today, Life, Logic, Money, Invest, Agents. Life and Money keep their sections. Pets, streak, ₹ wallet and level stay.
New: the dailiesThe app has no single view of "what did I do today". Each design adds five dailies: habits, expenses logged, mood, code solved, daily puzzle.
New: one plus buttonToday the only add button is on Money. Each design shows a different way for one plus to add an expense, note, reminder, task, habit check-in or solve.
WidgetsEach design has small, medium, large and lock-screen widgets. They need the iPhone app shell; the web app cannot have them.

A · Ember

Your day on one time rail. Reminders, expenses and notes all land on the same timeline, with five daily checks across the top. It keeps Forge's dark look and uses the flame colour only for what needs you now.

Plus button

Floats above the tab bar. Opens one text field: type "450 coffee with raj" and it becomes chips you can check. Type chips switch between expense, note, reminder and the rest.

Trade-off

The densest design. Typing an amount is slower than a keypad.

Principles at work

  • Isolation effect: only "next up" and overdue get the flame colour.
  • Recognition over recall: what you type becomes chips before saving.
  • Fitts's law: the plus sits in right-thumb reach on every tab.
  • Goal gradient: the 3 of 5 ring fills through the day.

B · Pinboard

Your day as coloured tiles. It grows the current Today tiles into a light, friendly board: one tile per part of your day, with the dailies as five circles on top. Each colour belongs to one kind of thing.

Plus button

Raised in the middle of the tab bar. Opens a grid of eight big tiles: expense, income, note, reminder, task, habit, solve, mood. "Repeat last" sits on top for one-tap logging.

Trade-off

Seven items in the bar is tight. The grid costs one extra tap every time.

Principles at work

  • Similarity: one colour per kind: yellow time, lilac money, blue logic, green notes.
  • Hick's law: eight clear choices, grouped in two rows, beat a long menu.
  • Spatial memory: tiles keep their place, so your thumb learns them.
  • Familiar patterns: centre plus, as in apps you already use.

C · Quiet

Your day reads like a page. One sentence tells you where the day stands. Below it, plain lists with thin rules and no cards. Numbers and titles use a reading typeface. The calmest of the four.

Plus button

A capture line that is always there, above the tab bar on every tab. Type anything. "450 coffee" becomes an expense, "call mom 6pm" a reminder, plain text a note. No type to pick.

Trade-off

You must trust the app to sort correctly. Less colour, so missed dailies shout less.

Principles at work

  • Reduction: type size and weight do the work of boxes and colour.
  • Tesler's law: the app takes on the sorting, so you only type.
  • Visibility: capture is always on screen, never behind a button.
  • Serial position: the summary comes first, where it is remembered.

Widgets

D · Rings

Your day as a score you close. Three rings, one per part of life: green for Life, violet for Logic, amber for Money. Today shows the rings, your week, and only what is still open. Built around the question you asked: did I do it today?

Plus button

Detached, beside a compact tab bar. It opens the right thing for where you are: a number keypad for an expense by default, with other types one tap away. An expense takes three taps: plus, amount, category.

Trade-off

Inactive tabs show icons only. A score can feel like pressure on a bad day.

Principles at work

  • Goal gradient: a nearly closed ring pulls you to finish.
  • Zeigarnik effect: Today lists only what is open, so it shrinks as you go.
  • Doherty threshold: the keypad opens instantly, with no system keyboard.
  • Smart defaults: today's date and your most-used categories come first.

Screens

Widgets

Only Today, the plus, Life and Money are drawn for each direction. Logic, Invest and Agents will follow the same pattern as the direction you pick. Widget check circles work without opening the app; every other tap opens the exact item.

My recommendation: D · Rings. Your goal is to see each day whether you logged expenses, solved problems and did your habits, and D is the only direction built around that one question. Its keypad is also the fastest way to log an expense. If icon-only tabs worry you, keep D and use A's labelled tab bar with the floating plus.

What I need from you

  1. Pick a direction, or a mix (for example "D, with A's tab bar").
  2. Confirm the five dailies: habits, expenses logged, mood, code (3 a day), daily puzzle. Tell me if reminders or tasks should count too.
  3. Two dailies need new tracking. The app does not record the day a puzzle was solved or whether expenses were logged today. Both are small additions.
  4. Plus default: should a tap open Expense first (as in D), or always show every type (as in B)?
  5. Widgets need the iPhone app shell, which needs a Mac with Xcode and an Apple Developer account. Everything else can ship in the web app first.
