Forge Capture Directions
Forge · design review · 15 Nov 2026

Three directions for notes, reminders and widgets

Same features, same sample day, three different ways to organise it. Each direction shows the Today screen, Reminders, Notes, quick capture, and its home-screen and lock-screen widgets. All content is sample data. The check circles work, so you can feel the interaction.

What PR #4 already hasNotes with tags, pins, 5 colours and nesting. Reminders with a title and time. Typed quick add that understands "tomorrow 6pm" and "in 2h". A Today home with 9 tiles.
What is missing for daily useToday shows no reminders or notes. Reminders cannot snooze or repeat. Alerts depend on adding to Calendar. Capture takes three taps to reach.
What widgets needAn installed web app cannot have widgets. They need the planned iPhone shell plus a small Swift widget. Widgets can check items off and open the app, but cannot take typing.

A · Ember

Your day is a timeline, and the app answers one question: what is next? Reminders and notes sit on one time rail. It keeps Forge's dark look and adds the flame colour from the app icon for the single thing that needs you now.

Choose it if

Your day runs on times. You want to open the app, see the next thing and close it.

Trade-off

The densest of the three. Notes are guests on the home screen, not the main act.

Principles at work

  • Isolation effect: only "next up" and overdue get colour.
  • Fitts's law: capture bar and check circles sit in thumb reach, 44pt targets.
  • Recognition over recall: what you type becomes chips you can check before saving.
  • Goal gradient: the 2 of 6 ring fills as the day gets done.

B · Pinboard

Everything is a card you can see at once. Notes are coloured cards on a board, using the five note colours Forge already stores. Reminders are white cards with large times. A plus button in the middle of the bar starts every capture.

Choose it if

You think visually and keep many short notes. You find things by where they are and what colour they are.

Trade-off

Fewer items fit on a screen. It is the furthest from today's code: new card grid, light theme first.

Principles at work

  • Similarity: colour always means "your note colour". Reminders stay white.
  • Hick's law: capture starts with three choices, and times are one-tap presets.
  • Spatial memory: cards keep their place, so you find them by position.
  • Familiar patterns: centre plus button and bottom tabs, as in apps you already use.

C · Quiet

Your day reads like a page. One sentence at the top tells you how the day stands. Below it are plain lists with thin rules and no cards. Notes look like writing, with a reading typeface for titles. The app works out whether you typed a note or a reminder.

Choose it if

You want calm. You write longer notes and dislike badges, colours and chrome.

Trade-off

Swipe actions must be learned once. Less colour means overdue items stand out less than in A or B.

Principles at work

  • Reduction: type size and weight do the work that boxes and colour do elsewhere.
  • Progressive disclosure: swipe for Later and Done; the same actions are in the detail view.
  • Serial position: the summary comes first, pinned notes last, where memory is strongest.
  • Tesler's law: the app takes on the sorting, so you only type.

Screens

Widgets

Widget notes: sizes follow iPhone small, medium and large. Check circles in widgets work without opening the app; every other tap opens the exact item. On tinted or clear home screens iOS removes colour, so each widget keeps its hierarchy through size and weight alone.

My recommendation: A · Ember for the app and widgets, with B's coloured cards as the layout of the Notes screen. A is the cheapest to build on PR #4 and its "next up" idea carries straight into every widget size. B's board is the better way to browse notes.

What I need from you

  1. Pick a direction, or a mix (for example "A, but Notes from B").
  2. Confirm the tabs. I assumed Today, Reminders, Notes and More, with habits, money, logic, invest and agents moved under More. The app has six tabs today.
  3. Approve the new behaviours shown: snooze, repeat, one-tap reschedule for overdue items, and reminders and notes on Today. None of these exist in PR #4.
  4. Widgets need the iPhone app shell. That needs a Mac with Xcode and an Apple Developer account. Everything else here can ship in the web app first.
