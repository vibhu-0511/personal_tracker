Forge Design Directions

Forge: three ways to capture your day

Notes, reminders and a home-screen widget, designed three different ways. Every screen uses the same sample day (Sunday 15 November) so you can compare like for like. Each board has a light and dark switch.

Direction A

Command

One list and one input. You type a thought into the bar under your thumb, and the app decides whether it is a note or a reminder from the words you used.

UX principles at work

  • Hick's law: one entry point for everything, so there is no "is this a note or a reminder?" decision before you type.
  • Fitts's law and thumb zone: the capture bar is wide and sits in the easiest reach area.
  • Jakob's law: behaves like the list apps you already know, so there is nothing to learn.

Best if

  • You capture many small things and care most about speed.
  • You like the current Forge look. This reuses its dark ocean theme and list rows.

Trade-offs

  • Least visual. Notes look the same as reminders at a glance.
  • Date parsing has to be right. A wrong guess costs trust, so the "understood as" chips are essential.
Direction B

Bento

Your day as a board of tiles. Each tile answers one question at a glance, and each tile is also a widget, so the home screen and the app look like the same product.

UX principles at work

  • Recognition over recall: colour, size and position tell you what a tile is before you read it.
  • Visual hierarchy: the next reminder is the biggest thing on screen; everything else is smaller.
  • Consistency: a tile in the app and the widget on the home screen are the same object.

Best if

  • The widget matters as much as the app, and you want to act without opening it.
  • You mostly check your day rather than write long notes.

Trade-offs

  • Fewer items per screen than a list. With 60+ notes you lean on search.
  • Capture is two taps (plus, then note or reminder) instead of one.
Direction C

Timeline

The day is one page read top to bottom. Reminders sit at the time they are due, notes sit at the time you wrote them, and a line marks now.

UX principles at work

  • Mental model match: a day already has an order. Laying things on time removes sorting and filtering.
  • Law of common region and proximity: the now line splits done from to-do without a single label.
  • Zeigarnik effect: unfinished items stay visibly below the line, which pulls you to close them.

Best if

  • You write longer notes and like looking back at what a day held.
  • Most of your reminders have a time, not just a date.

Trade-offs

  • Undated things (a someday list, reference notes) have no natural place and live only in Library.
  • Largest build. The timeline, editor and note-reminder link are all new screens.

My pick: B, with A's capture bar. You asked for a widget as a first-class feature, and Bento is the only direction where the app and the widget are the same design. Swapping its plus button for Command's type-anything bar gets capture back to one tap. Tell me A, B, C or a mix and I will build from there.

About the widget: a home-screen widget cannot be shipped from the web app alone. It needs the native iOS wrapper that the Forge plan already schedules. iOS widgets allow buttons and tick boxes but not typing, so every widget above uses only taps, and the plus buttons open the app straight into capture.
